## Project name

Still Here

## Challenge

Abandoned but not Forgotten

## High-level summary (short)

A free pixel game for kids. Drive the Apollo 15 rover on the Moon and the Opportunity rover on Mars, find the hardware NASA left behind, and learn what each piece did and why it mattered.

## Detailed project description

Still Here is a free, kid-friendly pixel game that runs in any web browser, on a phone or a laptop, with nothing to install. It is made for school-age children and anyone curious about space.

You play with just one button: hold the arrow to drive, let go to stop.

**Chapter 1: the Moon.** You drive the Apollo 15 Lunar Rover across the Moon. Along the way you find five things the astronauts left behind: the landing stage, the flag, the science station, the laser mirror and the rover's own sign. The buggy sometimes bounces in the low gravity. At the end you park it beside the two other rovers that stayed on the Moon: "You parked it. It is still there."

**Chapter 2: Mars.** You drive the Opportunity rover. Its battery is the only rule: driving uses power, and letting go lets the Sun charge the solar panel again. You find four pieces of Opportunity's landing hardware: the landing platform, the cone and parachute, the heat shield, and a real space rock that Opportunity found next to its own heat shield. Then the story jumps 14 years ahead. A dust storm darkens the sky, the wind sometimes cleans the panels, but the storm cannot be beaten. Opportunity falls asleep, and the game tells its true ending in a few short lines.

**Cards and album.** Each find opens a short card that says what the piece did and why it mattered, in simple words. You collect it with one tap, and it flies into your album. All 9 cards end up in the Album Hall. There you can tap any card to see it up close, read a longer story with "Read more", and open the source it came from.

**Patches.** Finishing a chapter earns a patch. Finishing both earns the golden "Still Here" patch and a small certificate.

There is no game over and nothing to fail: if the battery runs out, the rover simply rests until it is charged. It takes about 6 minutes to play. It works offline once loaded, collects no data, and saves progress only on the player's own device.

The tone is the theme: left behind, still working, never forgotten. Some of these things are still doing their job today: scientists still bounce lasers off the mirrors the astronauts left on the Moon.
